# AI支出再升級 為何輝達沒漲？市場資金輪動成關鍵

Alphabet([GOOGL-US](https://invest.cnyes.com/usstock/detail/GOOGL))公布優於預期的財報，同時大幅調升資本支出 (CapEx) 展望，顯示 AI 基礎建設投資將持續加速，市場原本預期將進一步帶動 AI 晶片需求。不過，輝達 ([NVDA-US](https://invest.cnyes.com/usstock/detail/NVDA)) 股價周四盤前反而下跌，市場資金轉向記憶體與晶片供應鏈業者，反映 AI 概念股出現新一波輪動。

![cover image of news article](https://cimg.cnyes.cool/prod/news/6544357/l/e5b10b1b3b1d120f9c2f578f53d301db.jpg)

（圖：REUTERS/TPG）

輝達盤前股價一度下跌 0.7%，與市場原先預期受惠於 Alphabet 擴大 AI 投資的情況形成反差。相較之下，美光 ([MU-US](https://invest.cnyes.com/usstock/detail/MU)) 盤前上漲 2%，SK 海力士則勁揚 5.2%，成為 AI 供應鏈中的主要受惠者。

市場分析指出，Alphabet 新增的資本支出將主要投入 AI 基礎建設，包括 AI 晶片、記憶體及相關資料中心設備，有助消除市場先前對 AI 硬體價格過高、企業投資熱潮恐難持續的疑慮。

理論上，AI 基礎建設支出增加應有利於身為 AI GPU 龍頭的輝達，但股價並未立即反映利多，主要原因在於市場資金配置策略正在改變。

近期市場趨勢顯示，部分投資人選擇賣出晶片股、轉進大型科技股。然而，隨著 Alphabet 證實 AI 資本支出仍將維持高檔，投資人開始重新布局 AI 供應鏈，尤其是專注於晶片與記憶體產品的業者，因此資金重新流向美光與 SK 海力士等公司。

分析認為，輝達雖然本業仍屬晶片設計公司，但隨著市值快速攀升，已成為全球市值最高的科技企業之一，市場逐漸將其視為「大型科技股 (Big Tech)」，而非單純的半導體公司。因此，在投資人進行資金輪動時，輝達反而成為部分資金獲利了結的對象，資金轉向市值相對較小、估值仍具吸引力的 AI 供應鏈個股。

除了輝達之外，Alphabet 本身股價也未因亮眼財報而上漲，盤前一度下跌近 5%，主因部分投資人擔憂公司持續提高資本支出，恐將壓縮未來獲利能力與自由現金流表現。

大型科技股整體表現同樣偏弱。亞馬遜 ([AMZN-US](https://invest.cnyes.com/usstock/detail/AMZN)) 盤前下跌約 2%，Meta Platforms([META-US](https://invest.cnyes.com/usstock/detail/META))盤前跌幅也約 2%，顯示即使 AI 投資前景更加樂觀，市場短線仍傾向將資金由大型科技權值股，重新配置至 AI 晶片、記憶體等供應鏈族群，以尋求更高的成長潛力。

NVIDIA continues to dominate the artificial intelligence chip market as we move through August 2026, with the company's data center revenue reaching unprecedented levels driven by relentless demand from cloud providers and enterprises racing to build AI infrastructure. The stock has experienced significant volatility following its most recent earnings report, reflecting investor debates about valuation sustainability versus the tangible reality of NVIDIA's technological moat in the AI accelerator space. While competition from AMD and custom silicon solutions from hyperscalers like Google and Amazon presents long-term challenges, NVIDIA's CUDA ecosystem and software dominance create formidable switching costs that protect its market position. Investors must weigh the company's premium valuation against its proven ability to execute on the AI transformation that is still in its early innings across global industries.

The semiconductor giant's latest quarterly results demonstrated that AI demand remains robust despite concerns about potential cyclical downturns. Revenue growth continues to outpace expectations, though the rate of expansion has moderated from the explosive levels seen in 2024 and early 2025. This normalization was inevitable as the company laps challenging year-over-year comparisons, but the underlying business fundamentals remain exceptionally strong. Management's guidance suggests sustained momentum through the remainder of 2026, supported by the ongoing ramp of the Blackwell architecture and strong backlog visibility extending multiple quarters into the future.

For investors seeking to capitalize on the AI revolution through semiconductor exposure, NVIDIA represents both the highest-conviction play and the most debated valuation story in the technology sector. The company's strategic positioning at the intersection of hardware dominance and software ecosystem lock-in creates a rare combination of growth and competitive protection that justifies continued attention despite the stock's elevated multiples.

## The Blackwell Architecture Transition

### Technical Specifications and Performance Gains

The transition to NVIDIA's Blackwell architecture represents the most significant product cycle in the company's history, with implications extending far beyond typical generational improvements. Blackwell delivers approximately four times the training performance and thirty times the inference performance compared to the previous Hopper generation, enabling AI models of unprecedented scale and complexity. The flagship B200 GPU incorporates 208 billion transistors manufactured using a custom 4NP process from TSMC, representing a substantial leap in silicon complexity and manufacturing sophistication.

The architecture introduces several innovations designed specifically for large language model workloads, including a new FP4 precision format that doubles effective memory bandwidth for inference applications. The second-generation Transformer Engine dynamically adjusts precision levels during training, optimizing both performance and accuracy for modern AI architectures. These technical improvements translate directly into total cost of ownership advantages for customers, as fewer GPUs are required to achieve equivalent computational outcomes compared to competing solutions.

Memory subsystem improvements represent another critical advancement, with Blackwell supporting up to 192GB of HBM3e memory per GPU at speeds exceeding 8 terabytes per second. This memory capacity addresses one of the primary bottlenecks in training large AI models, where parameter counts continue to grow exponentially. The NVLink interconnect has been enhanced to support 1.8 terabytes per second of bandwidth between GPUs, enabling efficient scaling across multi-GPU configurations that are essential for training the largest foundation models.

### Production Ramp and Supply Constraints

The production ramp of Blackwell has proceeded according to management's guidance, though supply constraints remain the primary limiting factor for revenue growth. NVIDIA has secured substantial foundry capacity commitments from TSMC, but the complexity of the Blackwell architecture and the advanced packaging technologies required have created natural constraints on production velocity. Lead times for high-volume orders continue to extend several months, with the largest cloud providers negotiating multi-year supply agreements to secure allocation priority.

The supply-demand imbalance has created a robust secondary market for NVIDIA GPUs, with resale premiums sometimes exceeding 50% above list prices for immediate delivery. This dynamic reflects the critical nature of AI infrastructure for technology companies competing in the generative AI space. NVIDIA has worked to address these constraints through expanded manufacturing partnerships and improved yield learning, though meaningful supply relief is not expected until calendar year 2027 when additional foundry capacity comes online.

For investors monitoring the Blackwell transition, key metrics include production yield rates, customer qualification timelines, and the pace of revenue recognition as the architecture reaches volume shipment levels. Management has indicated that Blackwell will comprise the majority of data center revenue by the fiscal fourth quarter, representing one of the fastest product transitions in the company's history. This rapid adoption underscores customer urgency in deploying the most advanced AI training capabilities.

## Competitive Landscape Analysis

### AMD's Growing Challenge

Advanced Micro Devices has emerged as NVIDIA's most credible competitor in the AI accelerator market, with its MI300 series products gaining traction among customers seeking supply diversification and potentially lower costs. The MI300X delivers competitive performance on certain inference workloads and has secured design wins with major cloud providers including Microsoft and Oracle. AMD's pricing strategy has been aggressive, with list prices typically 20-30% below equivalent NVIDIA configurations, creating meaningful economic incentives for customers to evaluate alternatives.

However, NVIDIA's software ecosystem advantage remains substantial and represents the primary competitive moat protecting its market position. The CUDA platform, developed over more than 15 years, has become the industry standard for GPU-accelerated computing, with millions of developers trained on its programming model and countless applications optimized for its architecture. AMD's ROCm software stack has improved significantly but still lags in terms of maturity, optimization breadth, and developer familiarity. This software advantage creates substantial switching costs that favor NVIDIA in competitive evaluations.

## **How Goldman Sachs Research estimates global AI investment**

Goldman Sachs Research augmented the frequently cited measurement of hyperscaler capex in several key ways: 

*   Our economists included capex projections for public companies other than hyperscalers in the US that are included in Goldman Sachs’ baskets of AI-related stocks. They also collected media reports of capex for key private companies that are pivotal in the AI ecosystem. 
*   Goldman Sachs Research added capex projections for AI-exposed companies outside the US. 
*   Our economists adjusted total capex spending by subtracting off 2022 capex levels for US hyperscalers. (This adjustment is motivated by our equity analysts’ views that nearly all incremental capex since 2022 has been allocated toward AI projects.) They similarly attribute all capex to AI for the private companies that are included. For less directly exposed and non-US companies, Goldman Sachs Research also assumes capex in excess of 2022 levels is AI related.  
*   Whenever possible, Goldman Sachs Research excludes financial leases that could otherwise be double-counted as hardware capex by other companies. 
*   To estimate where the investment takes place, Goldman Sachs Research leveraged data on the reported locations of announced investment projects (mainly from US hyperscalers). Our economists also assume that AI investment for most other non-hyperscalers (both private and global companies) follows a similar pattern, but that AI investment by major Chinese and Korean AI-exposed companies occurs outside of the US. 

Briggs notes that these estimates rely on some assumptions that are hard to verify. There is also some risk of double-counting capex for AI for some companies that do not report property, plant, and equipment investment separately from financial leases in their capex statements. Therefore, Goldman Sachs Research cross-checked its estimates against two other approaches. 

For a proxy for overall AI investment, our economists looked at gross profit realizations and forecast revisions relative to 2022 projections for public companies exposed to the AI buildout. They also used official government data to trace out the increase in nominal AI investment, and they used global trade data and the observed relationship between US imports and total AI investment to impute total investment in other economies.  

The two cross-checks imply a pace of investment remarkably similar to Goldman Sachs Research’s preferred measure of augmented hyperscaler capex. Each of the cross-checks suggests that AI investment will total around $1 trillion globally and just under $600 billion in the US in 2026.  

While the cumulative investment totals since 2022 implied by the different methodologies to prior years diverge a bit, on average they suggest that cumulative investment in AI will total $1.8 trillion by the end of this year, Briggs writes. 

## **What is the outlook for AI investment?**

“The AI capex growth outlook, including how high AI investment ultimately rises as a share of GDP and when capex growth slows, is a key source of uncertainty for macro markets right now,” Briggs writes.  

Goldman Sachs Research extrapolated its preferred estimates for AI investment to forecast total AI investment as a share of US GDP through 2028. Those estimates imply that AI capex will rise from 1.8% of GDP in the US (with global AI investment totaling 0.9% of global GDP) in 2026 to 2.5% of GDP in the US (1.3% globally) in 2027, with a further increase to 2.8% (1.4% globally) in 2028. 

“These levels are consistent with the 2%-5% of GDP peak investment impulses observed in prior general-purpose technology buildouts,” Briggs writes. “And while our US portfolio strategy team has flagged that consensus capex projections for 2027 are likely too conservative, even significant upward revisions would leave the level of AI investment as a share of GDP comfortably within the historical range observed in prior technology cycles.” 

To estimate when the growth in AI-related capex will slow, Goldman Sachs Research says a “dashboard approach is most appropriate.” Our economists compiled a broad set of leading indicators—including semiconductor manufacturing equipment imports in Taiwan and South Korea, relevant Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) indicators and components, import prices, and memory purchase and Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) rental prices—to check whether a slowdown is imminent. Our economists find that their selected indicators provide leading information about US AI capex growth.  

“The good news for the capex outlook is that all leading indicators rank near the top end of their range since 2022,” Briggs writes. “This pattern suggests a robust near-term growth outlook.”

Amazon, despite announcing higher capital spending, still had its stock price rise upon the release of the new quarterly figures, while investors reacted negatively to Alphabet's announcement of even more investments. Microsoft also saw its stock price soar, but had left cap ex guidance the same since April.

Meta, on the other hand, saw profits decline on legal expenses and severance costs in Q2. Revenue was still up. The company had in April said it would see capital expenditures between $125 billion and $145 billion this year. In its July statement, it raised the floor of the range to $130 billion. Shares also fell.

Some of the cost of depreciation associated with these large capital expenditures will not take a [bite out of profits until next year.](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/30/alphabet-will-spend-as-much-as-205-billion-this-ye/) While some investors are spooked by the scale of spending, the leaders of the companies involved are adamant that it’s the right way to go. “AI is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity where the current growth is unprecedented and the future growth even bigger,” Amazon CEO Andy Jassy recently wrote in his [annual letter to shareholders](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-ceo-andy-jassy-2025-letter-to-shareholders/). “We’re not going to be conservative in how we play this – we’re investing to be the meaningful leader, and our future business, operating income, and FCF (free cash flow) will be much larger because of it.”
