Founder STAR Chang blends a digital tech background with real-world coffee shop experience to turn raw SEC 10-K filings and the timeless wisdom of Warren Buffett, Benjamin Graham, and Peter Lynch into easy-to-digest guides you can read over a coffee break. ## About me, and why ValueCafe exists

Hey there, I'm **STAR Chang**, the founder and sole writer behind ValueCafe, though I like to call myself the editor-in-chief.

Before we dive in, let me be completely upfront about who I am. I'm **not** a Wall Street fund manager, I'm **not** a licensed financial advisor, and I'm **not** a day trader glued to screens all day. I'm just an independent, self-taught retail investor focused on long-term value investing. There's no massive team of analysts behind this site, either. It's just me and my trusty AI tools.

By day, I work in digital tech, which keeps me dialed into tech trends, stock movements, and macroeconomic shifts every single day. On top of that, I also run a brick-and-mortar coffee shop in real life.

### Why the name "ValueCafe"?

Running a coffee shop taught me that grabbing a cup of coffee is relaxing, comforting, and unhurried. And honestly, I think **investing should feel the exact same way**.

Too many people jump into the market and end up stressed out and anxious 24/7. But to me, brewing a great cup of coffee and sitting down at your own pace to read financial reports, market news, or a great book is a calm, enjoyable ritual, one that builds long-term wealth along the way. That's the whole idea behind **ValueCafe**: bringing together "Value" investing and that relaxed "Cafe" vibe, so you can easily absorb what seem like complicated business and financial concepts, all in the time it takes to finish a cup of coffee.

### My self-taught investing journey

When I first started investing, I was just like most everyday investors: overwhelmed by Wall Street jargon, dizzying stock charts, and conflicting financial headlines that made zero sense.

The real turning point came when I picked up Warren Buffett's biography, *The Snowball*. That book completely sparked my fascination with the stock market and business analysis. Soon after, Peter Lynch's *One Up on Wall Street* and *Beating the Street* became my guiding lights, showing me that regular retail investors can uncover incredible companies simply by using common sense and everyday observation.

As I dug deeper into the original writings and annual shareholder letters of Benjamin Graham and Warren Buffett, and started pulling up raw SEC filings myself, it finally clicked: evaluating a great company doesn't take advanced math. It takes everyday business sense, the ability to read the language of accounting, and the discipline to keep your emotions in check.

## My research and writing principles

Every guide, breakdown, and analysis published on ValueCafe follows three core principles.

1. **Primary sources first**  
   Every business teardown and financial report guide is grounded in official, first-hand filings (like SEC Form 10-K annual reports, 10-Q quarterly reports, and earnings call transcripts), backed by verified macroeconomic data (such as the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, the UK's Office for National Statistics, and credible industry reports), never second-hand market gossip.
2. **Business-owner mindset**  
   I treat every share of stock as a fractional piece of an actual business. That's why I focus on business models, free cash flow (FCF), return on equity (ROE), debt safety, and durable economic moats, instead of guessing where the stock price will go tomorrow or next week.
3. **100% independent and hype-free**  
   I don't pitch "get rich quick" stock tips, hype speculative penny stocks, or sell paid trading signals. To keep the site running independently over the long haul, I may occasionally recommend books or courses I've personally read, vetted, and found truly valuable, earning a small affiliate commission at no extra cost to you. I only recommend things I'd gladly spend my own money on.
