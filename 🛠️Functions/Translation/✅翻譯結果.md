Micron's $100B buyback ban is lifting with a 5x P/E, but watch out for the cyclical trap.

The two-year ban on share buybacks tied to the CHIPS Act for Micron (MU) officially wraps up on December 9. The board moved fast to raise its buyback authorization to $35.2 billion, and Wall Street is already hyping up a potential $100 billion repurchase spree by fiscal 2027, which could wipe out anywhere from 10% to nearly half its outstanding shares. Factor in a forward P/E of just 5x, and investors are getting seriously excited.

Right on cue, Japanese flash memory rival Kioxia dropped a cautious outlook, warning that flash memory price spikes are already losing steam. That alone sent Micron stock tumbling nearly 6% in a single day.

✅ Buybacks are all about capital allocation, not a magic floor for the stock price. Stock repurchases only build real wealth for shareholders when shares are trading below a conservative estimate of intrinsic value. Micron is already up more than 170% this year. If management spends top dollar buying back shares at the peak of the cycle, that cash is gone when the market turns, leaving zero cushion for a downturn.
✅ The memory market is in the middle of a K-shaped split. Crazy demand for AI server HBM is definitely eating up capacity, but everyday consumer gadgets like PCs and smartphones are still seeing a pretty sluggish comeback.
✅ Watch out for the classic low P/E trap with cyclical stocks. A 5x P/E looks like an absolute steal on paper, but cyclical companies always look cheapest right at the top of the boom when profits peak. If brand-new fab capacity floods the market by 2027, that bargain valuation might just be an illusion at the very top of the cycle.
