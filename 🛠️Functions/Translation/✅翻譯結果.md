A luxury cruise just went bankrupt in Cambodia, showing how soaring oil prices are reshaping the economy.

The luxury world cruise Villa Vie Odyssey was supposed to be a three-and-a-half-year journey hitting 150 countries. Instead, after just 750 days at sea, the operator went belly-up. Early one morning while docked in Cambodia, passengers woke up to an emergency notice telling everyone to pack up and get off the ship by 2:00 PM because the company was filing for Chapter 11 bankruptcy.

With geopolitical tensions heating up in the Middle East, marine fuel costs quadrupled. That caused monthly operating expenses to explode from $400,000 to over $2 million, completely crushing the company's cash flow.

This is definitely not an isolated case. The era of cheap energy and easy money is officially over. When the tide goes out, highly leveraged companies with zero pricing power and no cost hedges are in for a brutal wake-up call.

✔️ From airline tickets and freight rates to diesel fuel for American farmers heading into spring planting, energy inflation is trickling into food and everyday prices. Moody's estimates that sticky inflation combined with high interest rates is costing the average American family an extra $1,100 a year, only worsening the wealth divide in our K-shaped economy.

✔️ When you are dealing with energy-heavy operations and higher interest rates, financial discipline is literally what separates survival from disaster.
