# Facebook

Down big and suddenly calling yourself a "long-term investor"? Stop holding on blindly!

Honestly, "buy and hold" has to be one of the most misunderstood and abused concepts in the market today.

Sure, Warren Buffett famously said his favorite holding period is forever, but a lot of people overlook the first half of that quote: "when we own portions of outstanding businesses with outstanding managements." If what you're holding doesn't actually have a solid economic moat or trustworthy leadership, blindly holding on isn't discipline, it's just destroying your capital.

---

So when should you sell, trim, or rotate into something else?

1. Your thesis is broken: When a business faces structural decline, waiting to "break even" is just lying to yourself.
2. Extreme valuation borrowing from the future: When P/E multiples shoot near 100x and your margin of safety is completely gone, the risk-to-reward ratio turns terrible.
3. Opportunity cost: Your cash should always compete against better opportunities in the market. Never fall in love with a stock.

---

Most importantly: "Can you sleep at night?" If a 10% drop keeps you up, your position size is out of control.
